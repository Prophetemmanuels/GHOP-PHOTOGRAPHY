The four levers that decide your profit

  • Fuel efficiency. At K33.06/L your Fit at 12 km/L burns K2.76 of fuel per km. A well-driven 14 km/L Fit burns K2.36; a hybrid burns K1.50. On 1,000 km a week that is a K400–K1,260 swing from driving style and car choice alone. This is the single biggest lever you control.
  • Gross per online hour. Dead miles, long pickups and sitting in a jam earn you nothing. Aim for paid trips, not just online time.
  • Cost discipline. Fuel theft, unnecessary idling, late servicing and unpriced maintenance quietly eat the margin.
  • Target bonuses. Chase the weekly trip-count tiers — the bonus is pure margin because your fuel is already spent.

Reality check on the ads

Yango's own Zambia recruitment ads say car drivers in Lusaka and Kitwe can earn up to ZMW 5,000 a week. That is gross before commission, fuel, data, car wash, tyres and servicing — and it is a top-of-market figure, not an average.

Plan on keeping roughly 30–45% of gross as true profit in an efficient petrol car, less in a thirsty one and much less if you rent the car. Use the K3,000 Plan tab to see exactly what your numbers require.

Day 7

Sunday — off day, not a lost day

Sunday earns you nothing directly and protects everything else. The six working days only pay if the car is healthy, you are rested, and the week's numbers are known. Do these, in this order, and be finished by 14:00.

  • Sleep until you wake naturally — no alarm
  • Wash and vacuum the car inside and out
  • Check tyre pressures cold, top up if needed
  • Check oil, coolant, brake fluid, washer water
  • Fill the tank before the week starts (and before any price change)
  • Service items due this month? Book or do them today
  • Enter every day of last week into My Log
  • Compare actual net against the K3,000 target
  • Find your best-paying window and your worst — drop the worst
  • Set next week's fuel money aside in a separate place
  • Top up data bundle and phone credit for the week
  • Plan Monday's first three hours before you sleep
If you are behind target by Sunday, do not work Sunday. Add the hours to Friday evening next week instead — Friday pays roughly double a Sunday morning per hour, and you keep the rest day that keeps you safe.

What moved the price

  • Oil. International petrol and diesel benchmarks rose through September–October; the ERB cited Gulf tensions.
  • The kwacha. A weaker kwacha raises the local cost of every imported litre. November's hike came even though the diesel benchmark fell, purely on currency.
  • Tax. The three-month excise suspension and zero-rated VAT ended in September — that alone added over K6 a litre in October.
  • VAT returns 1 December. Unless extended, expect another step up in the December review.

How to buy fuel smarter

  • Fill before the last day of the month. Prices change at midnight after the ERB announcement. On a hike month a full 40 L tank bought early saves K50–K250.
  • Fill in the morning, cool fuel is denser. A small but real effect; you get slightly more energy per litre than at 3 pm heat.
  • Use one trusted station on your route. Avoid detours of 5 km just to save 20 ngwee — that detour costs you K2.50 in fuel.
  • Never run below a quarter tank. Sediment pickup and a dry tank cost far more than the fuel you saved.
  • Pay by card or mobile money where possible. It creates a record you can reconcile in the My Log tab.

Levers

If the target looks out of reach

Pull these, in this order

  • 1. Car efficiency. Going from 13 to 22 km/L cuts fuel per km from K2.54 to K1.50. Worth more than any other single change.
  • 2. Peak-hour density. Same hours, better placed — work 06:00–09:30 and 16:30–20:00 instead of 10:00–16:00.
  • 3. Cut deadhead. Reposition to where the next request is while you are still on the drop-off road.
  • 4. Weekly bonus tiers. The last 10 trips to hit a tier often pay double after fuel.
  • 5. Retail side. Airport runs, Comfort/Comfort+ requests and Yango Delivery/Cargo add higher-value jobs to the same tank of fuel.

Sanity checks before you commit

  • Is your gross per hour number real, or from your best day? Use a two-week average from My Log.
  • Are you counting tyres, brakes, clutch and servicing? At K0.55/km those are covered; at K0 they are a future bill.
  • If you rent the car, the daily fee comes off before profit. K300/day × 6 days = K1,800 — that changes everything.
  • Remember December VAT re-entry. Build the model at K34–36/L so a price hike does not bankrupt the plan.
Never confuse gross with profit. A driver grossing K7,000 a week and netting K1,500 is working for the fuel station.
Timing

When Lusaka pays best

Demand intensity by hour. Lusaka's congestion peaks are the money peaks: people who cannot walk or use a minibus call a car. The blue blocks are rest, not work.

Dead Fair Good Peak — be online Off / rest

The five peak windows

  • Mon–Fri 06:00–09:00. Residential → CBD. Home pickups in Kabwata, Chilenje, Matero, Chelston, Ibex Hill, Chalala, Meanwood, Avondale. Short hops, high volume, low deadhead.
  • Mon–Fri 12:00–14:00. Lusaka's lunch/midday crush — the CBD, Cairo Road, Great East and Great North all slow to a crawl. Riders who would walk it are now calling a car.
  • Mon–Fri 16:30–19:30. CBD → residential, the single richest window of the week. Position in the CBD by 16:15.
  • Fri–Sat 20:00–00:30. Nightlife: East Park, Arcades, Levy Junction, Cosmopolitan, Northmead, Kabulonga. Fewer drivers online, so higher paying requests.
  • Sun 09:30–13:30 and month-end. Church runs, market trips, and payday surges from the 25th to the 31st, when salaries land.

Conditions that pay a premium

  • Rain. Lusaka's rainy season (Nov–Apr) brings sudden downpours. Demand spikes and average speeds collapse, so per-minute fares climb. Work the first two hours after rain starts.
  • Month-end & payday. 25th–31st is the strongest week of the month for both volume and tips.
  • School terms. Term opening and closing weeks bring long intercity and airport runs.
  • Airport waves. Early-morning and evening KKIA arrivals; Comfort+ airport transfers start around K47 and run per km beyond that.
  • Public holidays & conferences. Agriculture and trade shows, weddings, funerals — all long trips at short notice.
  • When other drivers sleep. 05:00–06:30 is quietly good: commuters, early flights, almost no competition.

Rest is a profit strategy

  • Sleep 7 hours. A tired driver misses requests, drives badly, gets poor ratings and crashes. All three destroy earnings.
  • Take one full day off. Six days of peaks beats seven days of burnout — you cannot earn in a week where you are sick or the car is broken.
  • Rest in the trough, not in the peak. Between 10:00 and 15:00 on a Monday, sleep or eat. Do not burn fuel hunting.
  • Break every 4 hours. Stretch, water, food, toilet. Most accidents in this business are fatigue accidents.
  • Night safety. Verify the passenger name and destination, keep the car visible, do not accept off-app cash trips, tell someone your route on late runs.

Micro-habit checklist

  • Fill the tank the night before, not at rush hour
  • Tyres and fluids checked before going online
  • Phone at 100%, data bundle active, charger in car
  • Car clean inside and out, water bottles and tissue stocked
  • Positioned in the CBD by 16:15 on weekdays
  • Acceptance rate kept above your bonus threshold
  • Log the day before you sleep

Fit 1.3 — the things that actually cost you money

  • CVT fluid. If yours is the CVT, change the HCF-2 fluid roughly every 40,000 km. A slipping CVT takes you from 12 km/L to 9 — and then to a rebuild. This is the most expensive thing you can neglect on a GE6.
  • Keep it under 2,000 rpm. GE6 owners consistently report the 1.3 needs revs to move and drinks when you use them. Let the gearbox do the work, roll into stops, lift early.
  • Tyres at 210–230 kPa (30–33 psi). The Fit is light, so under-inflation hurts proportionally more. Check cold, weekly — it is worth up to 3% fuel.
  • 0W-20 oil and a clean air filter. Cheap items worth 2–4% economy and they protect the engine in stop-start heat.
  • AC discipline. You need it for ratings in 30°C heat, but full-blast AC in a 1.3 can cost 8–12% fuel. Vent the car first, then AC on low.
  • 42 L tank means frequent fill-ups. Fill the night before an ERB announcement, never in the middle of a peak. At K1,389 a tank, a badly-timed fill is real money.
  • Magic Seats are an income stream. Fold the rears and the Fit becomes a small van — Delivery by Car (from about K19) and Express Cargo (from about K60) pay far more per km than a passenger hop.
  • The 2013 age question. Yango's published guidance prefers cars under about 10 years old; yours is 13. Confirm eligibility with Yango Zambia or your station before spending on fitness, taxi insurance and licensing. Older cars get inspected harder — keep it spotless.

At K33.06 a litre, the difference between a 12 km/L Fit and a 22 km/L hybrid is roughly K1,260 a week at 1,000 km. That is the gap between missing and hitting a K3,000 target — but before you shop for a hybrid, note that how you drive the Fit is worth up to K400 of that on its own.

Buying rules for a Yango car

  • Four doors, working belts, under 10 years old — Yango's basic eligibility in Zambia.
  • Parts availability first. Toyota and Honda dominate Lusaka's spare-parts market; an obscure import with no local parts will eat your margin.
  • Hybrid battery check. On a used Aqua or Prius, have the battery health read before you buy. A replacement battery can cost more than the fuel you save in a year.
  • Ground clearance matters. Lusaka's untarred shortcuts and rainy season punish low cars.
  • Cooling system. Stop-start traffic in 30°C heat is brutal. A weak radiator means a blown head gasket and a dead week.
  • Insurance and fitness. Budget taxi insurance, road tax and fitness as fixed costs, not surprises.
Yango's own 2025 review named the Toyota Vitz the most-booked car on the platform in Zambia — cheap, simple, everywhere. But "most popular" is not the same as "most profitable per km". The Aqua usually wins that fight.

Operating manual

Make the app work for you

Getting on the road

  • NRC and a valid Zambian driver's licence (Class B or higher)
  • Vehicle logbook — or a signed owner agreement if the car is not yours
  • Valid insurance including taxi cover
  • Road tax and fitness certificate
  • Passport-size photo and a smartphone with Yango Pro (Android)
  • Taxi registration number and taxi licence where required
  • Background check and app account verified
  • Confirmed with Yango / your station that a 2013 Fit is accepted (age rule)
Requirements shift; confirm the current list with Yango Zambia or your taxi station before you pay anyone for paperwork.

Understand the money flow

  • Rides are paid out weekly. Bonuses are typically settled separately, often monthly. Plan your cash flow around the payout day, not the trip day.
  • You pay commission to Yango, plus a station fee if you drive under a fleet. The platform fee is small; the station's cut is set by the station, so negotiate and read it.
  • Ask, in writing, what the exact deduction is on your first payout. Compare it against the model in this app and correct the commission field.
  • Bonuses are tiered on completed trips in a period. A trip cancelled or refused can knock you out of a tier — the cost is far more than that one fare.
  • Keep a separate fuel account. The most common failure in this business is spending the fuel money on the weekend.

Beyond rides

Other ways the same tank earns

Yango Delivery (moto)

Lusaka rates run from about K10.17 minimum plus roughly K4.95/km. Fast turnaround, tiny fuel burn — but two-wheelers in traffic need real care.

From about K19 minimum, roughly K4.2/km. Pairs beautifully with mall runs when ride demand dips at 14:00.

From about K60 minimum, roughly K11/km, with ten free minutes of loading. Furniture and appliance moves pay far better per km than a passenger hop.

Earnings craft

Twelve habits that move the number

  • Protect your rating. Above 4.8 keeps you first in line for requests. Clean car, charged phone charger, correct music volume, no unsolicited conversation.
  • Never argue about the fare. Off-app negotiation gets you deactivated and costs you everything.
  • Use the passenger's name and confirm the destination before moving. Wrong-trip cancellations are pure loss.
  • End the trip where the next request is. Reposition empty only with purpose.
  • Learn three alternate routes for Great East, Great North and Kafue Road. Traffic knowledge is paid time.
  • Keep water, tissue and a phone cable. Cheap, and it shows up in ratings and tips.
  • Decline the trap trips. A 6 km pickup for a 2 km fare is a loss disguised as a job.
  • Work the bonus tier, not the single fare. Ten more trips to reach a tier often beats three premium rides.
  • Take your break at 10:00, not at 13:00. Rest in the trough, be online in the peak.
  • Bank a float. Two weeks of fuel money in reserve means a price hike never takes you off the road.
  • Track every day. The drivers who hit K3,000 know their numbers to the kwacha.
  • Service before it breaks. A breakdown costs more in lost earnings than in parts.
Risk

Safety and the things that end businesses

Avoid

  • Picking up street hails off-app at night, especially in unfamiliar areas.
  • Carrying large cash sums — bank or mobile-money the day's takings.
  • Driving uninsured or with lapsed fitness. One incident erases years of profit.
  • Letting a second driver use the car without an agreement in writing.
  • Working 14-hour days back to back. Fatigue is the leading cause of the crash that ends the business.

Protect

  • A written daily/monthly target, reviewed every Sunday.
  • Separate accounts: fuel money, maintenance money, living money.
  • The maintenance reserve of K0.50–0.60 per km, never touched.
  • A phone mount, charger and a second SIM for data redundancy.
  • Your own record of every trip, so a disputed payout is a conversation you can win.
